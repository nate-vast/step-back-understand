# Step Back Understand

1. **Anchor the thing being understood.** Run when the user explicitly invokes this skill or asks to understand a subject through what shaped it. Identify the person, work, idea, product or system and the aspect they want to understand. Inspect the subject itself or a representative work before tracing backward. Finish with a concrete subject and an open question, rather than a generic request to learn an entire field.

2. **Find what made it what it is.** Identify the relevant influences, teachers, earlier works, disciplines, components, experiences, collaborators or conditions. Treat user-supplied names as research leads, not proof of influence. Choose connections for explanatory value, not fame or proximity. Finish with a small working map of subject → shaping source → what that source may explain.

3. **Study the shaping sources themselves.** Read, listen to, inspect or otherwise engage with the relevant source material; a list of influences or a biography summary is not the result. Look for the actual technique, concept, constraint, experience or ingredient carried forward. Support each important connection with inspected evidence: first-person accounts, primary works, documented dependencies or credible history as appropriate. Distinguish documented influence, observable composition/dependency, interpretive comparison and unknown relationship. Similarity alone does not establish influence. Finish with a specific contribution and evidence for each connection used in the explanation.

4. **Follow depth where it improves understanding.** If a shaping source remains opaque, repeat the inquiry for what shaped that source. Keep the original subject visible while exploring. Adjust depth to the user's interest and the unresolved question; use no fixed generation count or permanent mastery threshold. Deeper understanding can be the deliverable even without an immediate practical payoff. Finish the current pass when you can explain the meaningful connections, or explicitly bound the unanswered branches when evidence or scope limits further study.

5. **Return to the original thing.** Explain how it absorbed, combined, changed or rejected those inputs and what remains distinctive about it. Tie the account to concrete features of the subject; avoid reducing a person or work to a sum of influences. If there is a current task, explain what this understanding changes about that task; if the purpose is learning, explain what the user can now recognize or understand. Finish with the subject, the important shaping connections, what they reveal, and any consequential uncertainty, with sources attached to the claims they support.

## Choose the right step back

Use this skill for understanding, including when nothing has failed. Keep `step-back-evaluate` for stalled work and repeated failed approaches. When that recovery process exposes a missing understanding of the subject's foundations, use this procedure to investigate it, then return the findings to the recovery decision. Invoking this skill does not add automatic routing or rewrite the recovery skill.

## Check the result

A useful result explains a connection that changes how the original subject is understood. A famous-name list, generic first-principles essay, unsupported family tree or endless chain that never returns to the subject needs another pass.

For a musician, study the candidate shaping artists' relevant work and the evidence of connection before attributing influence. For a product or system, inspect the constituent mechanisms and inherited design ideas before explaining the visible behavior. Use the same subject → shaping sources → richer understanding loop in either domain.
