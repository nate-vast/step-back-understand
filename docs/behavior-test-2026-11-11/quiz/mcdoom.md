# June McDoom's "Follow the Light": Comprehension Quiz

Answer each question in 2-4 sentences.

1. The label's notes say this is "not a reggae or dub record, not a folk record." What is the album's basic recipe instead? Say which tradition supplies the bottom of the sound, which supplies the top, and name at least two specific sounds you would hear on the record.

2. Describe how a song on this album gets written and arranged: what it starts as, what rule McDoom set for herself this time, and how she and Evan Wright turn the starting point into a full arrangement. Why do writers say even the instrumental parts feel "sung"?

3. How was the album recorded and mixed? Cover where the drums came from, where and on what the rest was tracked, how McDoom deals with capturing her own voice, and what happened during mixing.

4. Why does this album fuse folk harmony with Jamaican bass and rhythm at all? Explain where each half comes from in McDoom's life and what made her decide to put them on the same record.

5. Why is the album called "Follow the Light"? Say who or what the "light" is, and what McDoom says the songs turned out to be about, including when she realized it.

6. What had McDoom released before this album, and how did the album arrive (label, timing)? What does the album add to the sound of her earlier work?

7. How did critics receive the album? Name what reviewers consistently praised and the main reservation that came up.

8. A few days after a full-band festival set, McDoom plays the same songs in a record shop: alone, unplugged, just voice and guitar, with no drummer and no backing track. Using what you know about how the album is built, predict what the songs will sound like in that setting, what will be missing, and why the songs still work. How would you expect her to feel about the difference?
