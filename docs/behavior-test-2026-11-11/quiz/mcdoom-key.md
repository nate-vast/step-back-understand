# June McDoom's "Follow the Light": Answer Key

Album: *Follow the Light*, June McDoom's debut full-length, released October 16, 2026 on Jagjaguwar. 12 tracks.

Scoring: each question is worth 2 points (16 total). Every fact below was read in the cited source on 2026-11-11. "Label notes" means the album description on McDoom's Bandcamp page.

Two places where sources differ. Do not mark an answer down for either version:
- Studio: the label notes say she and Wright built the record "in the studio they keep at home." Rolling Stone met her at a studio in Greenpoint, Brooklyn that she shares with Wright and Nick Hakim.
- Her father: the label notes and Rolling Stone call him a carpenter who built his own speakers and DJ'd at home. Clash calls him a "soundman" and Song Bar calls him a "sound-engineer and DJ."

## 1. The basic recipe (how it works: sound)

**Model answer:** The album lays folk harmony over Jamaican rhythm: the label notes call it "the harmony of one laid over the rhythm of the other." The bottom is dub and reggae, with sturdy basslines pushed to the front of the mix, off-beat chops, echo, spring reverb and dub sirens. The top is folk: McDoom's high, airy voice stacked into dense harmonies over nylon-string guitar, harp and strings. The whole thing sits in a dreamy, reverb-heavy haze that reviewers compare to shoegaze and Cocteau Twins.

**Rubric:**
- 2 points: Says Jamaican music (dub/reggae) supplies the bass and rhythm and folk supplies the harmony and voice, and names at least two real sounds from the record (for example: prominent dub bass, dub siren, echo or spring reverb, layered vocal harmonies, harp, nylon-string or finger-picked guitar, strings).
- 1 point: Gets the two-tradition split right but names no specific sounds, or lists sounds without saying which half does what.
- 0 points: Calls it simply a folk record or simply a reggae record, or describes a sound not supported by the sources.

**Sources:**
- https://junemcdoom.bandcamp.com/album/follow-the-light (label notes: "harmony of one laid over the rhythm of the other"; dub and Wall of Sound in the low end; Cocteau Twins)
- https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/albums/june-mcdoom-intrigues-with-the-dub-reggae-folk-hybrid-of-follow-the-light (basslines "front and centre in the mix"; multilayered vocals; harp with dub bass on "Without You")
- https://diymag.com/review/album/june-mc-doom-follow-the-light (spring reverb, dub sirens, psych-folk strings, dense vocal harmonies on "Dream Dub")
- https://loudandquiet.substack.com/p/follow-the-light-to-the-celestial-folk-reggae-and-dub-of-june-mcdoom (dub siren and triangle, then finger-picked guitar, at the start of "Human Angel")

## 2. Writing and arranging (how it works: writing, arrangement, players)

**Model answer:** Nearly every song began as just McDoom's voice and a nylon-string guitar, with no thought yet for other instruments. For this album she set a strict rule to get the lyrics right first and feel strong about them before any other step. Then she and her partner Evan Wright stack parts one at a time: she sings each part she hears (a bass line, the top line of a piano part), he plays it and fills out the rest, and he plays most of the instruments while she layers the vocal harmonies. Because the parts all start as melodies she sang, the instruments move like voices.

**Rubric:**
- 2 points: Has the starting point (voice and guitar), the lyrics-first rule, and the method of McDoom singing parts for Wright to play (or Wright playing most instruments while she layers harmonies), and links that method to the "sung" quality.
- 1 point: Has two of those three elements, or has all three but does not explain why the parts feel sung.
- 0 points: Describes a full band writing together, an outside producer shaping the songs, or otherwise misses the voice-and-guitar start and the McDoom/Wright method.

**Sources:**
- https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/features/interviews/june-mcdoom-is-writing-her-own-history ("I write on a nylon string"; "they're all vocal melodies"; "I'll sing the top line of what I like the part to be, and then he fills out the rest")
- https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/june-mcdoom-follow-the-light-interview-1235644687/ ("a really strict rule that I wanted to be really intentional about the lyrics ... before any other process"; "'I really want this bass line,' and I sing it and then he'll play it"; "McDoom produces her own projects")
- https://junemcdoom.bandcamp.com/album/follow-the-light ("Nearly every one began as just her voice and a guitar"; Evan "playing most of the instruments while June layered in the vocal harmonies")
- https://13thfloor.co.nz/june-mcdoom-follow-the-light-jagjaguwar-13th-floor-album-review/ ("Many of the instrumental parts also began as melodies she sang")

## 3. Recording and mixing (how it works: production, voice)

**Model answer:** They started by cutting live drums with Victor Axelrod (the producer who records as Ticklah) in his Sunset Park basement. McDoom and Wright then built everything else themselves in their own studio, tracking to both tape and digital, and went to a friend's house to record the harp. Her voice has a lot of air in it and no single microphone catches all of it, so she blends several mics. Mixing was slow and exacting: twelve-hour days, and she started the mix over after her parents told her at Christmas that they couldn't hear enough of her vocals.

**Rubric:**
- 2 points: Gives at least three of these four: (a) live drums recorded with Victor Axelrod / Ticklah; (b) the rest self-recorded by McDoom and Wright in their own studio on tape and digital (harp at a friend's house counts here); (c) multiple microphones to capture her airy voice; (d) long mixing days and restarting the mix after her parents said the vocals were too low.
- 1 point: Gives one or two of the four, or says only that it was a slow, handmade, self-produced record made with Evan Wright.
- 0 points: Says it was made quickly, live in one room, or handed to an outside studio team; or gives nothing verifiable.

**Sources:**
- https://junemcdoom.bandcamp.com/album/follow-the-light (live drums with Victor Axelrod in Sunset Park; "tracking to tape and digital both"; harp at a friend's house; twelve hours a day mixing; restarting after the Christmas visit)
- https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/features/interviews/june-mcdoom-is-writing-her-own-history ("a strange voice to capture because there's a lot of air in it"; mixing multiple microphones)
- https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/june-mcdoom-follow-the-light-interview-1235644687/ (works with tape and a computer; eight-track reel-to-reel, Ampex preamps, Roland Space Echo in the studio; "I wanted the record to feel kind of handmade even if it sounded not-perfect")

## 4. Why the fusion (why)

**Model answer:** Both halves are her own history. She grew up in a Jamaican household in South Florida where her father built speakers and ran sound systems at home, so reggae and dub bass came through the walls, and her grandfather, a mento musician, put a guitar in her hands. Folk she found alone, through things like *The Sound of Music* and Simon and Garfunkel in *The Graduate*, while her parents called it "that sleepy music." For years the Jamaican music felt like her family's rather than hers; while finishing her EP she got deep into rocksteady and 1960s-70s Jamaican records (Alton Ellis was the trigger) and decided her next project would join that rhythm and bass with the folk harmony she loves. She also wants to introduce listeners to Jamaican music they don't normally hear.

**Rubric:**
- 2 points: Explains both origins (Jamaican music from her family and home; folk discovered on her own) and gives a reason for joining them (coming back to older Jamaican records such as Alton Ellis and rocksteady and wanting them in her next project, or wanting to bring all parts of herself and introduce people to Jamaican music).
- 1 point: Gives the family background but not the folk side or the decision, or says only "it reflects her heritage" without specifics.
- 0 points: Treats the mix as a marketing or genre exercise, or attributes it to an outside producer or the label.

**Sources:**
- https://junemcdoom.bandcamp.com/album/follow-the-light (father's three sound systems; bass through the bedroom floor; "that sleepy music"; grandfather Colin Smith, mento; the Alton Ellis record)
- https://loudandquiet.substack.com/p/follow-the-light-to-the-celestial-folk-reggae-and-dub-of-june-mcdoom ("When I was finishing my EP, I was getting really into older Jamaican music ... I knew that I wanted to find a way to incorporate that into my next project")
- https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/june-mcdoom-follow-the-light-interview-1235644687/ ("I wanted to do some sort of fusion between the genres, but I wasn't really sure how"; "I kind of found folk music on my own"; "trying to find myself through my music by including different parts of myself")
- https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/features/interviews/june-mcdoom-is-writing-her-own-history ("I want to introduce people to the music that I'm really nerdy about")

## 5. Why the title, and what the songs are about (why)

**Model answer:** The "light" is the people who love you showing the way. The label notes put McDoom's grandmother Aggie at the centre of the record as her guide ("Memory" was written with her in mind), and the title track began with an image of McDoom on a dark, rainy road following a light. McDoom says the songs all ended up being about not holding on to people who don't accept you and finding the people around whom you can be free. She did not plan that: she knew the sound she wanted before she knew what the album would say, and only saw it as a positive record about finding yourself after it was finished.

**Rubric:**
- 2 points: Identifies the light as loved ones or family guiding her (grandmother Aggie, or the dark-road image, or "the family member you really want to call"), states the theme of finding yourself and the people who accept you, and notes she only recognized the theme afterwards.
- 1 point: Gets either the meaning of the light or the theme, but not both; or gets both but claims she set out with that concept.
- 0 points: Gives a generic reading (religion, optimism in general) with no link to family or self-acceptance.

**Sources:**
- https://junemcdoom.bandcamp.com/album/follow-the-light (grandmother Aggie as "the core of all of this"; "Memory" written with her in mind; "People who love you showing the way")
- https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/june-mcdoom-follow-the-light-interview-1235644687/ (dark, rainy road image; "all the songs ended up being about that"; "I still wasn't even really thinking about it until the record was done"; knew the sound before she understood what the album would say)

## 6. What came before (context)

**Model answer:** McDoom began releasing music in 2022 with a self-titled debut EP of hushed folk, followed by the *With Strings* EP in 2023, which includes her cover of Judee Sill's "Emerald River Dance." *Follow the Light* is her first full-length and her first release on Jagjaguwar, which she signed to when the album was announced in August 2026; it came four years after the first EP. What the album adds is the Jamaican half: the deep bass of dub and reggae brought into the intimate folk productions of the EPs.

**Rubric:**
- 2 points: Knows there were earlier EPs starting in 2022 (naming the self-titled EP or *With Strings* is enough), that this is the debut album and her first on Jagjaguwar, and that the new element is the dub/reggae bass and rhythm.
- 1 point: Has the release history or the label but not what changed in the sound, or the reverse.
- 0 points: Thinks this is her first release of any kind, a second album, or that the earlier work was already reggae or dub.

**Sources:**
- https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/june-mcdoom-follow-the-light-interview-1235644687/ (self-titled debut EP, 2022, "hushed brand of folk"; the album "brings the deep bass of Jamaican dub into McDoom's intimate productions")
- https://loudandquiet.substack.com/p/follow-the-light-to-the-celestial-folk-reggae-and-dub-of-june-mcdoom (debut album "four years after her first EP release"; 2023's *With Strings* EP and the Judee Sill cover)
- https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/news/june-mcdoom-signs-to-jagjaguwar-and-details-debut-album-follow-the-light (signing announced 4 August 2026; "follows two early EP releases"; first new music on Jagjaguwar)

## 7. Reception (context)

**Model answer:** Reviews were strongly positive. Critics kept returning to the same thing: the folk and dub/reggae hybrid sounds natural rather than like a trick, held together by her voice, harmonies and careful, patient production. DIY gave it 4 stars out of 5 and called "Dream Dub" the album's mission statement, The Line of Best Fit gave it 7/10, and Qobuz featured it as a Qobuzissime pick and called it a masterpiece. The recurring reservation is that the lushness can be too much: The Line of Best Fit said that when the songs land less hard the careful craft starts to "resemble gorging on candyfloss," and Song Bar said a few arrangements are so luxuriant that their details compete for attention.

**Rubric:**
- 2 points: Says reception was positive, names what was praised (the genre hybrid feeling natural, plus voice/harmony or production), and gives the reservation about over-sweetness or over-lush arrangements. A specific score or outlet is welcome but not required.
- 1 point: Says it was well received and names the praise, but has no reservation; or gives the reservation without what was praised.
- 0 points: Says it was panned or ignored, or invents scores or outlets.

**Sources:**
- https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/albums/june-mcdoom-intrigues-with-the-dub-reggae-folk-hybrid-of-follow-the-light (7/10; "gorging on candyfloss"; "an artist boldly operating in their own sphere")
- https://diymag.com/review/album/june-mc-doom-follow-the-light (4 stars; "no showcase or musical party-trick"; "Dream Dub" as mission statement)
- https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/magazine/story/2026/10/19/qobuzissime-june-mcdoom/ (Qobuzissime feature; "a masterpiece exceeding the expectations preceding it")
- https://www.song-bar.com/album-releases/june-mcdoom-follow-the-light-album-review ("A few arrangements become so luxuriant that their details compete for attention")
- https://www.clashmusic.com/?p=463796 (Clash: the album sounds "incredibly natural and unified")

## 8. The solo in-store set (application)

**Model answer:** Stripped to voice and guitar, the songs will sound like quiet folk songs, because that is how nearly all of them began before any arranging. What goes missing is everything added in the studio: the Jamaican half (dub bass, drums, echo and sirens) and the stacked vocal harmonies, harp and strings. They still work because the lyrics and melody were settled first and the arrangement was built on top of a complete song. McDoom would notice the difference and be a little uneasy about it, since she cares about every part, but she has said a drummerless set has "a folkier vibe" and that she is trying to accept the live show "can be its own thing."

**Rubric:**
- 2 points: Predicts a folkier, quieter result; identifies that the bass/drums/dub side and the layered harmonies or harp are what drop out; and explains why the songs survive (they start as voice and guitar, with lyrics and melody fixed before arrangement). Her likely attitude is a bonus, not required.
- 1 point: Predicts "more folk, less dub" but gives no reason from how the songs are built, or gives the reason without saying what is lost.
- 0 points: Predicts the songs fall apart without the production, or that the set would sound the same as the record.
